## Item #1 — Sweep 2 & Sweep 4 anomalies  ✅ fully explained

### The original framing was wrong about the cause

HANDOVER said sweep 2's 4D-bar argmax (28) was "−13" off the "LSF reference"
(41), and sweep 4 had a suspicious monotonic decrease.

Three independent problems were found, none of them an atlas registration bug:

**(a) The "LSF reference" shots were filesize argmaxes.**
`scripts/diag_reference_origin.py` reads the tile-sharpness per-shot CSVs for
each sweep and finds the centre-tile focus argmax for each metric:

| sweep | hardcoded "LSF ref" | = filesize argmax | true `lsf_mtf50` argmax | maze argmax |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sweep1 (M0.385) | 137 | **137 ✓** | 138 | – |
| sweep2 (M1.0) | 41 | **41 ✓** | **28** | 29 |
| sweep3 (M2.0) | 47 | **47 ✓** | **30** | 32 |

All three "references" match the **filesize** argmax exactly, none match the
LSF/maze focus argmax. The label "LSF-pipeline reference" in
`task15_4d_bar_lsf_all_sweeps.py` / HANDOVER / README is a misnomer. JPEG
filesize is a poor focus proxy (it keeps climbing past focus with noise/texture);
for sweep 2 it peaks 13 shots *after* best focus.

**(b) MTF50 saturates at M≥1.0 → degenerate argmax.**
At M=1.0 the 4D bar's edge is sharp relative to the pixel pitch, so `lsf_mtf50`
flat-tops at ~0.337 cy/px across the whole in-focus region (the clamp the
`lsf.py` docstring warns about for scribes). The argmax then picks an arbitrary
shot in the plateau. Non-saturating metrics (Laplacian-variance, Tenengrad,
edge acutance) all give a clean single peak instead — see
`diag_metric_compare.py`. This also corrupted sweep 3's *reported* "success":
its old MTF50 argmax was 45–46 (saturation), but the true bar focus is ~28–30.

**(c) The field has a real focus tilt.**
`scripts/diag_field_curvature.py` measures Laplacian-variance focus at fixed
boxes across the field (one consistent pipeline). Sweep 2:

| probe x | 666 | 1486 | 2807 | 4128 | 5448 | 5600 | 6769 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| focus shot | 30 | 30 | 30 | 28 | 24 | 21 | 21 |

A clean left→right march (~10 shots edge-to-edge): the plane of best focus
sweeps across the frame as the rail advances (wafer/sensor not square to the rail
axis). The 8D bar (right side) genuinely focuses ~7 shots before a centre
feature — that part of the old "gap" is real optics, correctly reported by the
atlas. Sweep 3 shows the same tilt (left 28 → right 35).

### Corrected result (`scripts/task16_corrected_focus.py`)

Per-shot bar tracking + non-saturating focus metric + the *true* LSF/maze
reference:

| sweep | atlas centre focus | true LSF centre | maze centre | old MTF50 argmax | old "ref" (filesize) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (M0.385) | 136 | 138 | – | 137 | 137 |
| 2 (M1.0) | 28 | 28 | 29 | 28 (plateau) | 41 |
| 3 (M2.0) | 28–32 | 30 | 32 | 45–46 | 47 |
| 4 (newer) | 0 (clipped) | – | – | 1 | – |

**The atlas-anchored measurement agrees with the real LSF/maze focus pipeline
to within ~2 shots at every magnification (0.385–2.0)** — a stronger and more
accurate result than HANDOVER claimed, once the two metric/reference bugs are
removed.

### Sweep 4 — not a bug

Every non-saturating metric puts sweep 4's focus at **shot 0**: the bar is
visibly crispest at the first frame and monotonically defocuses (see
`sweep4_newer_rig_roi_col4.png`). The rail started at/near focus and walked away,
so the true peak is at or before the sweep's first shot (clipped). There are also
exposure/illumination step-changes around shots ~40 and ~80 (visible in the ROI
crops and as discontinuities in every metric). Atlas behaviour is correct; this
is an acquisition issue.

### Recommended code change

In `task15_4d_bar_lsf_all_sweeps.py`: (1) use a non-saturating focus metric
(lapvar/Tenengrad on the tracked bar box) for the **argmax**, keeping `lsf_mtf50`
only as the absolute-resolution readout; (2) rename `lsf_ref_shot` →
`filesize_ref_shot` and add the true `lsf_mtf50`/`maze` argmax from the CSVs as
the real reference; (3) report focus per in-frame instance to expose the tilt.

## Item #2 — Autonomous registration (task #14)  ⚙️ core bug fixed; absolute labelling re-scoped

### The old morphological path was broken, not merely ambiguous

`out/registration/method_morphological_results.json` shows anchor-RMSE of
**6,186 / 10,120 / 6,377 px** (sweeps 2/3/4) and fit-residuals of 21–275 px —
i.e. the auto-generated correspondences were internally inconsistent, far worse
than the "off-by-a-few-dies absolute-label ambiguity" HANDOVER described.

**Root cause: a rotation-sign error in the de-rotate→un-rotate coordinate
mapping** (the code comment "apply rotation by +tilt (NOT −tilt)" was wrong).
`scripts/reg_auto.py --validate` maps the known anchors into the de-rotated frame
and shows they land on detected scribes within **0–9 ds-px with sign −1**, vs
20–60 ds-px with the old +1.

### Geometry fix proven (`reg_auto.py --oracle`)

Auto-detect scribes, assign labels via the known affine, **re-fit from scratch**
using only the auto-detected scribes:

| sweep | auto cols/rows | oracle anchor-RMSE | OLD morph anchor-RMSE |
|---|---|---|---|
| sweep2 | 6/4 | **141 px** | 6,186 px |
| sweep3 | 3/3 | **46 px** | 10,120 px |
| sweep4 | 5/3 | **37 px** | 6,377 px |
| sweep1 | 7/2 | 503 px (weak detection) | (failed) |

A 40–220× improvement. Residual ~40–140 px is downsample (1024-px) detection
precision; the manual full-res 3-anchor method reaches 2–8 px. So with correct
labels, autonomous registration is now geometrically sound.

### Absolute labelling — the genuinely hard part, re-scoped

The remaining gap to full autonomy is assigning the **absolute** (cl, rl) without
the anchors. Two findings reshape HANDOVER's plan:

1. **Phase (mod-4) is only recoverable when the wide-column / tall-row pitch is
   inside the FOV.** Sweep 4's visible rows (rl 2–4) are all "short" — no tall
   row between them — so phase cannot be read from spacing alone.

2. **The handover's whole-vs-jagged idea is not robust at these magnifications.**
   `reg_auto.py` implements silicon/edge-visibility disambiguation, but:
   - sweep 4 is **fully interior** (wafer fills the entire frame, no edge/corner
     visible — see `sweep4_newer_rig_regexplore.png`), so there is no jagged die
     to find;
   - the background detector gives **false positives** on dark die features
     (sweeps 3/4 report a "right edge" that isn't there) and **false negatives**
     on sweep 2's real corner cuts (dark corners carry JPEG texture, so they
     fail the "dark AND smooth" test).

   Net: edge/jagged anchoring only works when a clean wafer edge is in view,
   which is the minority case for M≥1 sweeps.

**Recommended path (replaces whole-vs-jagged as primary):** distinctive-feature
anchoring. The 4D bar uniquely identifies **row D** (the only row ≡3 mod 4 in
A–F) and **col ≡0 mod 4**; combined with the now-correct geometry it pins the
absolute offset for *any* FOV containing a 4D-type bar — including fully-interior
ones. The existing template method already detects whole-die templates; pairing
template-match positions with the corrected mapping is the cleanest route to full
autonomy. (Feature detection wants full-res, not the 1024-px downsample, so the
anchor step should run on a crop at predicted scale.)

### Status

Delivered: corrected geometry (the blocking bug), phase recovery where the
wide/tall pitch is in view, edge-visibility disambiguation for edge-containing
FOVs, and an end-to-end validated pipeline. Not delivered: robust absolute
labelling for fully-interior / no-wide-pitch FOVs — needs the feature-anchor
above. `reg_auto.py` (no args) prints the current autonomous result and flags the
ambiguous cases honestly.

## Item #4 — Atlas-anchored bond-pad measurement (POC C)  ✅ both problems solved

POC C (`tile-sharpness/scripts/poc_wide_feature_inventory.md`) searched each tile
of the M=0.385 sweep for an "isolated wider feature" as an MTF50 target. It
failed on **57% coverage** (content-dependent: dense bond-pad/text tiles had no
monolithic band) and **non-determinism** (percentile-threshold drift moved the
detected feature shot-to-shot; LSD ordering flipped detection run-to-run).

`scripts/task17_bondpad_atlas.py` replaces the blind search with atlas-anchored
sampling: register the sweep once, project all 36 bond-pad components (per die
type) to fixed sub-pixel locations across every in-FOV die, and read a focus
metric at each on every shot.

| | POC C | Atlas (pad-FFT) | Atlas (Laplacian-var) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coverage | 57% | **100% (142/142)** | **100% (142/142)** |
| Usable focus (SNR≥1.8) | unstable | 7% | **87% (124/142)** |
| Focus argmax | flips between adjacent shots | median 128 | **median 138, IQR [132,138]** |
| Determinism | no (LSD/threshold) | yes (fixed locations) | yes (fixed locations) |

- **Coverage** rises 57% → 100%: every atlas-known bond-pad location yields a
  reading regardless of content.
- **Determinism** is by construction — no re-detection, no percentile thresholds,
  no LSD ordering, so output is identical run-to-run.
- **Focus quality depends on the metric** (same lesson as item #1): the pad-FFT
  amplitude is depth-of-field-broad at low M (7% usable), but Laplacian-variance
  on the *same* atlas-anchored ROI gives clean, tightly-clustered focus curves —
  87% usable, all peaking at the true focus (138). See
  `out/diag_2026_06/task17_bondpad_atlas.png`.

Both stated POC C problems are solved. The recommended low-M sharpness fallback
is **atlas-anchored bond-pad ROIs scored by gradient energy**, not pad-FFT and
not blind feature search.

## Cross-cutting finding

The same theme runs through all three items: **frequency-domain / threshold
metrics (JPEG filesize, MTF50 near saturation, single-frequency pad-FFT) are
unreliable focus discriminators here; simple gradient-energy metrics
(Laplacian-variance, Tenengrad) on atlas-anchored ROIs are robust, non-saturating,
and deterministic.** Wherever the pipeline currently takes an argmax for focus,
gradient energy is the better signal.
